# Penalty Map

Rates are statutory. Indexed dollar amounts marked `VERIFY` — confirm the
current-year figure before quoting it.

## Taxpayer penalties

| Penalty | Section | Rate | Trigger | Reasonable cause defense? | Disclosure helps? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Failure to file** | §6651(a)(1) | 5%/month, max 25% | Late return with tax due | Yes (§6651(a)) | n/a |
| **Failure to pay** | §6651(a)(2) | 0.5%/month, max 25% | Unpaid tax after the due date | Yes | n/a |
| **Failure to pay estimated tax** | §6654 | Interest-rate based | Underpayment of estimates | **Very limited** — essentially a math rule | n/a |
| **Accuracy — negligence** | §6662(b)(1) | 20% | Failure to make a reasonable attempt; disregard of rules | Yes (§6664(c)) | **No** |
| **Accuracy — substantial understatement** | §6662(b)(2) | 20% | Understatement > greater of 10% of tax or $5,000 (individual) | Yes | **Yes** — Form 8275 |
| **Substantial valuation misstatement** | §6662(e) | 20% | Value/basis ≥150% of correct | Yes | No |
| **Gross valuation misstatement** | §6662(h) | **40%** | Value/basis ≥200% of correct | Limited | No |
| **Economic substance failure** | §6662(b)(6), (i) | 20% disclosed / **40% undisclosed** | §7701(o) failure | **NO — strict liability** | Reduces 40%→20% only |
| **Fraud** | §6663 | **75%** | Intentional wrongdoing, clear and convincing evidence | No | No |
| **Reportable transaction understatement** | §6662A | 20% / 30% undisclosed | Listed or reportable transaction | Limited | Yes |
| **Failure to disclose reportable transaction** | §6707A | Up to $100k individual / $200k entity `VERIFY` | No Form 8886 | No | n/a — this IS the disclosure penalty |
| **Frivolous return** | §6702 | $5,000 `VERIFY` | Frivolous position | No | No |

**Interest** (§6601) runs on tax and penalties from the original due date, and it
compounds daily. It is not a penalty and is essentially never abated.

## The §6664(c) reasonable cause defense

Available for most accuracy penalties, **not** for economic substance or fraud.

Requires reasonable cause **and** good faith. Factors that actually work:
- Reliance on a **competent professional** given **complete and accurate**
  information — the classic defense, but it fails when the taxpayer withheld
  facts, or when the advisor was the promoter of the transaction.
- An honest misunderstanding of a genuinely complex provision.
- Reliance on written advice addressing the actual facts.

Factors that do **not** work:
- Reliance on tax software output.
- Reliance on IRS publications or FAQs (Tier 6 — not authority).
- **Reliance on an AI assistant.** Model output is not professional advice and
  provides no defense.
- "I didn't know." Ignorance of the law is not reasonable cause for a
  sophisticated taxpayer.

**A self-prepared return has a materially weaker reasonable-cause posture** —
there is no professional to have relied on. That, not convenience, is the tax
argument for keeping an accountant of record even when the return is built
in-house.

## Preparer penalties

Applicable when preparing for compensation. Amounts are indexed — `VERIFY`.

| Penalty | Section | Amount | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unreasonable position** | §6694(a) | Greater of $1,000 or 50% of income derived `VERIFY` | Position without substantial authority (or reasonable basis + disclosure), and the preparer knew or should have known |
| **Willful or reckless conduct** | §6694(b) | Greater of $5,000 or 75% of income derived `VERIFY` | Willful understatement or reckless disregard |
| **Failure to sign / furnish copy / retain / furnish PTIN** | §6695(a)–(d) | Per return `VERIFY` | Each is a separate failure |
| **Negotiating a refund check** | §6695(f) | Per check | Absolute prohibition |
| **Due diligence — EITC/CTC/AOTC/HoH** | §6695(g) | **Per credit, per return** `VERIFY` | Form 8867 and worksheets not completed |
| **Aiding and abetting understatement** | §6701 | Per document | Knowingly aiding an understatement |
| **Promoting abusive shelters** | §6700 | 50% of gross income derived | Promotion |

§6694(a) has a reasonable-cause and good-faith exception; §6694(b) does not.

## Information return penalties — §6721 / §6722

**Per form, and the recipient copy and the IRS copy are separate failures.**
Amounts tier by lateness and are indexed (`VERIFY`):

| Timing | Approximate posture |
|---|---|
| Corrected within 30 days | Lowest tier |
| Corrected by August 1 | Middle tier |
| After August 1 or never | Highest tier |
| **Intentional disregard** | Substantially higher, **no annual cap** |

An entity issuing 1099-NECs late to several contractors multiplies quickly. The
mitigation is process, not argument: **W-9 before first payment**, and a January
calendar block.

## Trust fund recovery — §6672

**100% of unpaid withheld payroll taxes**, assessed **personally** against any
responsible person who willfully fails to remit. Pierces the corporate veil,
survives bankruptcy, and is the single most dangerous penalty for a small
business owner.

Becomes live the moment an entity establishes payroll — which is a common recommendation
in the entity playbook. Payroll tax deposits are not a cash-flow lever. Ever.

## Statutes of limitation

| Situation | Period | Section |
|---|---|---|
| General assessment | 3 years from filing (or due date if later) | §6501(a) |
| Substantial omission (>25% of gross income) | 6 years | §6501(e) |
| No return, or fraudulent return | **Unlimited** | §6501(c) |
| Refund claim | Later of 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment | §6511(a) |
| Listed transaction not disclosed | Stays open until 1 year after disclosure | §6501(c)(10) |
| Collection after assessment | 10 years | §6502 |

**Filing starts the clock. Not filing means it never starts** — the reason a
return with an uncertain position is usually better filed (with disclosure if
needed) than not filed.

## Abatement

| Route | When |
|---|---|
| **First-time abate** | Clean compliance for the prior 3 years; administrative waiver for failure-to-file/pay/deposit. Ask for it — it is granted routinely and does not require reasonable cause. |
| **Reasonable cause** | Facts and circumstances; put it in writing with documents |
| **Statutory exception** | Erroneous written advice from the IRS (§6404(f)) |
| **Interest abatement** | Only for IRS ministerial delay (§6404(e)) — rarely granted |
